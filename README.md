## What is an AI Forward Deployed Engineer?

An **AI Forward Deployed Engineer** is a customer facing engineer who deploys AI and large language model products inside a client's environment, adapts them to real workflows, and owns the outcome end to end. The role blends four skill sets:

- **Software engineering**: shipping production code, APIs, data pipelines, and integrations.
- **AI and machine learning**: working with LLMs, retrieval augmented generation (RAG), agents, evaluations, prompt engineering, and fine tuning.
- **Solutions and consulting**: scoping problems with customers, running pilots, and translating business needs into technical designs.
- **Communication**: explaining tradeoffs to executives and engineers, writing clearly, and driving adoption.

Companies hiring for this role or close variants include Palantir, OpenAI, Anthropic, Scale AI, Sierra, Glean, and a growing list of AI startups. Titles vary: Forward Deployed Engineer, Forward Deployed Software Engineer, AI Solutions Engineer, Deployment Engineer, and Applied AI Engineer.

## Why this repository exists

I am preparing for AI Forward Deployed Engineer interviews and documenting the full process in public. This repository collects the study material, practice problems, frameworks, and reflections that helped me, so other candidates can prepare faster and with less guesswork.

Use it to:

- Understand what the AI Forward Deployed Engineer interview loop looks like.
- Follow a structured study plan across coding, system design, and AI topics.
- Practice customer facing scenarios, take home projects, and behavioral stories.
- Learn from a real prep journey with dated notes and retrospectives.

## The interview loop at a glance

A typical AI Forward Deployed Engineer loop includes some mix of:

1. **Recruiter screen**: motivation, background, and role fit.
2. **Coding interview**: practical problem solving, often data manipulation, parsing, or API work rather than hard algorithm puzzles.
3. **System design interview**: designing an AI powered system, data flow, and integration with a customer stack.
4. **Take home or live build**: a small project using an LLM API, evaluated on code quality, product sense, and communication.
5. **Customer facing scenario**: a role play where you scope a problem with a mock stakeholder and propose a solution.
6. **Behavioral interview**: ownership, ambiguity, conflict, and customer empathy.
7. **Final round**: onsite or virtual panel combining the above with team fit.

Details differ by company. See [`company-guides/`](company-guides/) for specifics.
